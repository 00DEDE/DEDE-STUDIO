Case Study · 001

UNESCO

A rebranded identity system for the world's cultural heritage agency that is both western and eastern minded, alive and ever present.

UNESCO safeguards over eleven hundred sites across one hundred and sixty-seven countries. Behind every site is a story, a people, a piece of our shared humanity that deserves to be carried forward. I wanted this redesign to reflect that responsibility. To create something that could embrace the richness of our world without asking every culture to fit the same mold. An identity that could feel just as alive in a Nairobi campaign as in a Kyoto exhibition, while honoring what makes each place, and the people who call it home, unique.

I began with the wordmark as an anchor and built the identity outward. I asked myself, what do we all have in common? What is innately in us? Stories! Our stories are embedded in us, and we embed those same stories into our textiles through weaving and repetition of shapes. When I realized that, the system flourished and continuously expanded. Through the woven identity, I created a modular iconographic system, a color palette shaped by human experience, and interactive components for a digital archive that became part of the larger visual language. Every element works together like a true symphony, not one note is forgotten or left behind. To me, preservation is not only about protecting what we have inherited. It is about making sure the stories, languages, and traditions that shape us have a future, and that generations after us can still discover what makes our world extraordinary.

Interactive Atlas

To celebrate UNESCO's 80th year anniversary, I proposed the construction of an “interactive atlas” that would encourage the audience to activate different parts of the world and by doing so, they would witness just how interconnected we all are in the fabric of human experience and how crucial it is to protect what makes us human.

01Foundations

Across our existence, we have done one thing. Embed our stories, our pasts, and our futures into textiles.

The rebranded logomark for UNESCO is at its core, 4 interlocking Us coming from all 4 directions of our world. Each U stands for the areas that UNESCO protects: Traditions. Languages. Ecosystems. Monuments. Without these 4 pillars of human history, I argue neither you nor I would have the identity we have today.

02Palette

A color palette that celebrates what our eyes can see and what our feet have walked upon since our inception. Planet Earth.

6 colors. 2 groups. Blue for knowledge, green for life, and yellow for culture. Then there is ash grey for memory, brown for ancestry and rich dark brown, I called “deep earth” for heritage. This is human expression (vibrancy of life) and human experience (hues of our Earth) coming together to be the fabric of human existence.

03Voice

A system that is a continuous reminder for the public and a call-to-action.

The typography is direct and inclusive. This is most evident in the proposed slogan and secondary taglines. “The World Needs U” is a play of words. Yes it is the first letter of UNESCO, no coincidence there but further than that, this slogan is meant to communicate to the world how much better this life is with you in it.

The secondary taglines, “Safeguard Humanity's Inheritance” and “Protect What Makes Us Human” are the call-to-actions. It is our turn to do our part in forging the way forward to the future but with our heritage on our backs. A tree's roots must grow deep into the Earth for its branches to reach into the skies.

04Applications

05Iconography

Due to the system being made in a modular manner, it facilitated being able to create custom icons for the 4 areas that UNESCO protects.

Each group: Traditions, languages, ecosystems and monuments have their own respective icons that are meant to both look and feel like the zones they are representing. Then to add even more human interconnection to the rebrand, the logo gave me a couple of “friends.”

06Celebrating 80 Years

I chose to make printed tangible matter that spoke to 80 years of UNESCO accomplishments: that is human connection.

I commissioned a woven blanket to be made, and to get in on the fun I made a hand sewn vegetable tanned leather bag that had all the icons form an image of our world. This same image appears on the printed atlas book. This was the predecessor to the interactive atlas. You never know where one small idea might take you.
