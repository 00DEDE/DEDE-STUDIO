Case Study 05

Trader Joe’s

01Overview

The creation of a hypothetical rebrand for Trader Joe’s that feels just as eccentric and fun as they do.

Trader Joe’s is loved by many, myself included. Yet its current branding feels dated, borrowing motifs from Polynesian culture without any real connection to the islands. I set out to create a rebrand and line extension more in line with its contemporary aesthetic and energy. No longer old and expected, the branding becomes just as exciting as the products themselves, all while keeping the offbeat whimsy that makes Trader Joe’s, so beloved.

02Foundation

A friendlier, cooler Joe.

The current Trader Joe’s logo is fun, but it pushes the quirk too far. Its irregular counters and high-contrast terminals feel exaggerated, and being set in all uppercase makes it less personal than it should be.

But Trader Joe’s is in its own lane. It does its own thing. It is not a traditional grocery market. The rebranded wordmark is careful not to push it into an oversaturated lane, while paying closer attention to how each character’s terminals end and to the consistency of its strokes.

The new logo does not strip away all Trader Joe’s has built. Rather, it uplifts its inviting essence while bringing in a contemporary edge.

03Concept

Products that spark conversations between grandparent and grandchild.

My main goal for this rebrand was to present a unified line extension that creates dialogue and bonding between two generations: baby boomers and Generation Alpha. Grandparent and grandchild. These products are educational, adventurous, and full of childlike wonder.

That is perhaps the heart of this project, what was pumping through its veins. Childlike wonder. A moment where adult and child feel not so different from one another, and for a moment, speak the same emotional language.

Alongside the products, I designed an outdoor activity kit to be sold at Trader Joe’s. It includes iconic American pastimes: two-person paddleball, two-person pickleball, a frisbee, a coloring book with colored pencils, and custom die-cut stickers. The idea is for families to create memories at the park again. Memories that will stay with the children long after, when they are the age their grandparents are now.

04The Full Lineup

Meet the family.

Meet the family: Joey the orange juice, Julie the apple juice, Chloe the strawberry gummies, and Louie the sweet banana chips. Each character has their own hero product, and together they come home on one cereal box: Tiny Fruity Cuties.

My favorite is Chloe. Her “freckles” are the seeds of a strawberry.

To bring the rebrand and this new world to life, I carried the characters onto the activity products. One is playing paddleball, another becomes the face of a frisbee.

Family is important to me. At the time of this project, my family was going through a loss, and I was away from them. This project was made in their honor. Each character represents a member of my family.

It is vital to my process to give purpose to a project, no matter how big or small. The project is a person, not an extension of me. I must talk to it and bond with it so I can understand what we can give each other.

05Deliverables

A more complete look at the whole picture.

Each character and asset was hand-drawn, then scanned, redrawn in Illustrator, and color corrected. Whenever there is a chance to go analog, it excites me. Pencil and paper. There is nothing more intimate than that in the process of design.

Not every asset made it into the final deliverables, but that does not mean the time was wasted. Exploration is important to me. When telling a story, every possibility must be considered and contemplated. The story told to the public is the concise narrative of an endless amount of research and consideration.

06Identity System

A world seen through Good Guy Joe’s eyes.

This is perhaps my most youthful project: imaginative, tangible, and maximalist. I am open-minded and versatile. I am not bound to one style. One thing that defines my work is my affinity for color. The world is beautiful, and I have been given the gift of experiencing its colors.
