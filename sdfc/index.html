Case Study 02

San Diego
Football Club

01Overview

The creation of an identity system where the communities of San Diego feel truly represented.

San Diego FC, or SDFC for short, made its Major League Soccer (MLS) debut in 2025. Its branding was met with controversy from the moment it was unveiled. Rather than leading with a traditional color, the club chose chrome as its hero color, because chrome reflects the colors of the community around it. It is a great sentiment, but in practical terms, chrome presents real challenges in digital and especially in print.

What makes this team interesting is that it had no prior history, so this could not be a legacy rebrand. Others might see that as a pain point, but no history gave me full creative control to present an organization focused on the future. That is the anchor of this rebrand: a future-forward professional soccer team that only moves onward, hand in hand with the communities ready to support it.

This is SDFC.

02Foundation

An identity rooted in the community. A team uplifted by the community.

SDFC’s current logo is well made. Its 18 lines represent the 18 communities of San Diego County, and I love the storytelling. But it has not resonated with the very communities it represents. A story only matters when the people it is about can see themselves in it.

This rebrand asked me to dig deep into research and make connections before attempting to redesign the logo and identity as a whole. It is imperative to my process to understand the brief and ask the right questions, so that I never impose empty design choices.

03Logo Reveal

A team with no stats and no trophies.

What should exist around its logo? What should the world of SDFC look like? Its communities.

San Diego is rich with diversity in its neighborhoods, ecosystems, cultural backgrounds, and personal interests. This logo reveal shows the backbone of the team: the community.

The stats will come. The trophies will come. But first, the identity must make sure no one gets left behind, and that the people of San Diego see themselves in the badge.

04Logo Breakdown

United under one badge.

If the double-crested cormorant could speak, it would call San Diego its home. This captivating bird has striking teal-blue eyes and jet-black plumage, and it became the motif across the brand, starting with the logo.

Each wing carries five feathers, one for each of San Diego County’s five districts, and together they read as hands reaching for the sky. One wing is intentionally larger than the other, creating the visual of the cormorant diving beneath the water to catch its prey, and representing an adult’s hand and a child’s hand. This is the past and the future. The big and the small. The old and the young, uniting under one badge.

Finally, as an homage to the artistry found all across San Diego, the letters are given blunted terminals, reading as calligraphic forms inspired by urban art.

05Palette

A hue palette that carries both onward adventure and essence of home.

The hues chosen for this rebrand were inspired by members of San Diego’s ecosystem that exist with each other and for each other. The coastal waters of San Diego and the Cormorant that feeds its kin from those same waters.

06Typeface Usage

A system that displays both expression and practicality.

Radio Grotesk (Regular and Bold) serves as the “workhorse,” used for body copy and when the brand’s voice is informative.

Manuka (Medium) Majuscule serves as the primary typeface, used for both headlines and taglines and when the brand’s voice is direct.

Beast Mode (Regular) Majuscule serves as the hero typeface, used specifically with ongoing campaigns and when the brand’s voice is most expressive and unruly.

07Photography

An intentional approach to how every visual connects back to the system.

It was important to me that the imagery encapsulate the communities of San Diego. The spotlight is shared between every culture, every age group, and every community.

The photography hones in on a specific moment in time. By doing this it becomes direct, unapologetic and inspirational.

08Graphic Elements

The Cormorant continues to be a point of reference and inspiration.

The shapes are derived from the corners of the “S” and “D” in the hero logo. While studying the double-crested cormorant, I noticed the thin feathers at the back of its head. These are called filoplumes, and I was drawn to their thinness. I carried that thought back into the system: the graphic elements for the most part appear as strokes, with no fill, and are enlarged so they feel like visual guides throughout the composition.

18 cities. 5 regions. 1 club.

The slogan calls to mind the numbers that make up San Diego County as a whole. 18 unique cities. 5 distinct regions, each with its own style and flair. Then, the number 1. Read in sequence, it narrows. First, the audience sees themselves as part of a big group, then a smaller one, and finally, they see themselves. Just one person. Just one leaf on a tree of many leaves.

Welcome Home.

This secondary tagline honors the club’s debut season. San Diegans waited years for an MLS club of their own, and now they have a home away from home. A place where San Diego becomes one: Snapdragon Stadium.

Daygo, born & raised!

Many San Diego locals call their city “Daygo.” This tertiary tagline instills pride in the community by bringing forward a nickname no one else can claim.

09Campaign Activation

SOUL TIDE 2025.

To bring this rebrand to life, I created a campaign that flips the usual approach. Instead of inviting people to the stadium, the club goes out to the beaches of San Diego. Together, club and families collect the trash discarded along the shore, and that trash is then turned into recycled-material merchandise: products designed to come full circle. The event is filled with giveaways, contests, and memories made together.

I named it Soul Tide. It plays on the well-known expression “soul tied,” swapping the “d” and “e” so the ocean comes to everyone’s mind.

SDFC is much more than a club. It is part of a multicultural, multifaceted community. 1 team. 1 people. 1 goal.

Using SolidWorks and a CNC machine, I created wooden models of the merchandise that would be sold and given away on the day of the campaign. I also designed name tags, bottle packaging, woven trash bags, recyclable buckets, and wearables.
